Easy Walks Near Paris

Web Content Strategy project, 2026. With a partner, I designed and built a WordPress travel site that helps students, expats, and visitors find easy, beautiful nature walks they can reach from Paris by public transport.

Type
Product and content strategy project, WordPress website
Scope
MVP with 6 core pages and 12 curated walk guides
Tools
WordPress (Inspiro theme), PRD, SEO, UX writing, AI workflow design

The question

Many people in Paris want fresh air but do not know where to go without a car. Existing hiking resources feel too technical, too scattered, or not written for newcomers, and transport instructions, difficulty, and practical tips are rarely in one place. How do you make choosing an easy day trip feel effortless?

What I did

We started with a product requirements document. It defined the problem, the target audience (international students, new expats, and visitors), three personas, the MVP scope, and what was deliberately out of scope, such as route planners or technical GPX maps.

From there we designed the information architecture: a Homepage that explains the promise in one screen, a Start Here page for first-time visitors, an All Walks archive filterable by vibe, season, and travel effort, an About page, and a reusable walk guide template. Every guide follows the same structure: a one-sentence promise, a quick facts block (travel time, walking time, difficulty, best season), step-by-step transport from Paris, what to expect on the route, practical tips, and a nearby cultural stop.

To keep 12 guides and future ones consistent, I built a custom AI skill using structured prompt frameworks (RISEN and TIDD-EC), with our Forêt de Chantilly page as the editorial gold standard. Given only a destination name, it produces a complete walk page with all content sections, SEO metadata, image suggestions, and formatting notes for the WordPress theme.

Key decisions

  • Easy walks, not hiking. The tone, imagery, and labels stay simple, warm, and reassuring, so the brand never drifts toward demanding treks.
  • Answer the hidden question. Every page answers "Can I realistically do this from Paris without stress?", and every inspiring description is followed by practical reassurance.
  • Consistent labels. Difficulty, duration, and travel effort use the same wording on every guide, so walks are easy to compare.
  • Prioritised requirements. Functional requirements were ranked Must, Should, and Could, with clear acceptance criteria, such as making the value proposition clear on the first mobile screen.
  • Measurable success. We defined metrics such as click-through from the homepage to guides, engaged time and scroll depth on guide pages, return visits, and social follows.

About the live site
