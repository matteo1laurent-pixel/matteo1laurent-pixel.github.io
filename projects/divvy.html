Divvy bike-share analysis

SQL and Power BI analysis of a Chicago bike-share database, focused on subscriber growth, usage, and revenue.

Type
SQL and dashboard project
Data
Divvy database: trips, users, and stations
Tools
SQL, Power BI

The question

How are subscribers growing, how intensively do they use the service, and what revenue does that represent? I answered these questions with SQL, then turned the results into Power BI dashboards.

What I did

I extracted and transformed the data with SQL, using aggregations, date functions, joins, and set operations. I then built Power BI dashboards showing monthly acquisition, growth rates, and the most active users.

Key findings

  • Revenue estimate. Assuming a subscription of $30 per user per year, I estimated revenue from active users, meaning users with at least one trip. There were 500 active subscribers in both 2024 and 2025, or about $15,000 a year.
  • Usage grew while the base stayed flat. Trips rose from 69,990 in 2024 to 130,010 in 2025, so each subscriber used the service much more.
  • Acquisition peaks. I measured new registrations per month. January and February 2024 were the strongest months, with 28 new users each.
  • Data quality. I found a group of users with no registration month, caused by missing registration dates.
  • Growth dynamics. I calculated month-over-month changes in new users, in absolute terms and as a growth rate, and highlighted the strongest periods in Power BI.
  • Power users. I ranked the 10 most active users by total distance traveled to identify who drives the highest usage.
